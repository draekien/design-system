# Laws of UX against the design rules

## Question

Which of the 30 laws on [Laws of UX](https://lawsofux.com/) back a rule in `docs/contexts/design-rules.md`, which point to a rule the system lacks, and which fall outside its scope of disclosure and composition?

This document covers the 21 laws not cited in `docs/references/ux-references.md` when it was written. Under ADR 0006, a gap listed here becomes a rule only when a story needs it. The Outcome below overrides the verdict of every law it adopts.

## Verdicts

| Law | Verdict | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Doherty Threshold](https://lawsofux.com/doherty-threshold/) | Gap | Loading states are in scope, but no rule says how soon a surface shows feedback after an action |
| [Postel's Law](https://lawsofux.com/postels-law/) | Gap | Would govern which input formats a field accepts; the registry has no input component yet |
| [Law of Similarity](https://lawsofux.com/law-of-similarity/) | Candidate citation | Could back "each fact is its own column and lines up down the list" in `list-rules.ts`; not cited because Precise already gives the reason |
| [Law of Common Region](https://lawsofux.com/law-of-common-region/) | Candidate citation | Could back drawing a card's facts inside one border; no card rule states why the card has a border |
| [Working Memory](https://lawsofux.com/working-memory/) | Candidate citation | Could back peek, which shows a record's context beside the list so the caller need not remember it |
| [Law of Uniform Connectedness](https://lawsofux.com/law-of-uniform-connectedness/) | Candidate citation | Could back the constellation's connectors on the docs site; that is a page rule, not a component rule |
| [Goal-Gradient Effect](https://lawsofux.com/goal-gradient-effect/) | Candidate citation | Could back how a meter shows progress; no story needs a progress rule yet |
| [Choice Overload](https://lawsofux.com/choice-overload/) | Not cited | Backs the same two-action limits as Hick's Law |
| [Mental Model](https://lawsofux.com/mental-model/) | Not cited | Backs "Follow Linear first" for the same reason as Jakob's Law |
| [Miller's Law](https://lawsofux.com/millers-law/) | Not cited | Its use would be a count limit, and a law is never the source for a value |
| [Serial Position Effect](https://lawsofux.com/serial-position-effect/) | Not cited | The menu rules put destructive actions last; the law says the last item is remembered best, which is not a reason the rules give for that placement |
| [Occam's Razor](https://lawsofux.com/occams-razor/) | Not cited | Overlaps Quiet, which already cites Cognitive Load |
| [Law of Prägnanz](https://lawsofux.com/law-of-pr%C3%A4gnanz/) | Out of scope | Concerns how shapes are perceived, not what a surface shows or how parts assemble |
| [Aesthetic-Usability Effect](https://lawsofux.com/aesthetic-usability-effect/) | Out of scope | Concerns visual appeal; visual treatment is governed by `DESIGN.md` |
| [Paradox of the Active User](https://lawsofux.com/paradox-of-the-active-user/) | Out of scope | Concerns onboarding and help content |
| [Cognitive Bias](https://lawsofux.com/cognitive-bias/) | Out of scope | Names no layout or disclosure consequence |
| [Flow](https://lawsofux.com/flow/) | Out of scope | Describes a state of the caller, not a part of a surface |
| [Parkinson's Law](https://lawsofux.com/parkinsons-law/) | Out of scope | Concerns task duration |
| [Peak-End Rule](https://lawsofux.com/peak-end-rule/) | Out of scope | Concerns how a whole journey is remembered |
| [Tesler's Law](https://lawsofux.com/teslers-law/) | Out of scope | Concerns where complexity is placed between product and caller |
| [Zeigarnik Effect](https://lawsofux.com/zeigarnik-effect/) | Out of scope | Concerns unfinished tasks being remembered |

## What the evidence favours

Doherty Threshold is the gap most likely to need a rule: loading states are in scope and no rule covers them. Write its story first.

## Outcome

Adopted on 2026-11-25: each law in the Laws of UX table of `docs/references/ux-references.md` backs a rule in `docs/contexts/design-rules.md`, tied to an existing story, and is checked by its Laws of UX tests. Scope now covers a law's consequence on a single surface and excludes journeys across surfaces. Every verdict above for a law not in that table stands.
